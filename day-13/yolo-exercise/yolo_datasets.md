# YOLO Dataset Recommendations  

Here are some recommended datasets that are open-source, beginner-friendly, and suitable for hands-on projects.

---

## 1. COCO (Common Objects in Context)
- **Description**: Large-scale dataset with 330K images and 1.5M object instances across 80 categories.
- **Use Case**: General object detection tasks.
- **Link**: [COCO Dataset](https://cocodataset.org)

---

## 2. PASCAL VOC
- **Description**: One of the earliest benchmark datasets for object detection with 20 categories.
- **Use Case**: Great for beginners due to smaller size and simpler structure.
- **Link**: [PASCAL VOC Dataset](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/)

---

## 3. Open Images Dataset (Subset)
- **Description**: Google’s dataset with millions of annotated images across 600 categories. Subsets are commonly used for YOLO training.
- **Use Case**: Students can work on a reduced subset to avoid storage issues.
- **Link**: [Open Images Dataset](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/web/index.html)

---

## 4. Custom Dataset – Fruits and Vegetables
- **Description**: Small dataset with common fruits and vegetables, annotated in YOLO format.
- **Use Case**: Easy-to-train dataset for learning YOLO basics quickly.
- **Link**: [Fruits Dataset on Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/moltean/fruits)

---

## 5. Street View House Numbers (SVHN)
- **Description**: Dataset of house numbers from Google Street View.
- **Use Case**: Digit detection, useful for number recognition tasks with YOLO.
- **Link**: [SVHN Dataset](http://ufldl.stanford.edu/housenumbers/)

---

## 6. Self-Created Dataset
- **Description**: Students can capture images (e.g., everyday objects, classroom items) and annotate them using tools like [LabelImg](https://github.com/heartexlabs/labelImg).
- **Use Case**: Helps students understand the end-to-end pipeline of dataset creation, labeling, and YOLO training.

---

## Recommendation 
- Start with **PASCAL VOC** or a **custom fruit dataset** for simplicity.
- Once confident, try **COCO subsets** for real-world applications.
- You can **build their own dataset** for a complete learning experience.
